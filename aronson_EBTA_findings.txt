
ARONSON - EVIDENCE-BASED TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
Structured Findings for FX Algo Trading (EURUSD/GBPUSD H1)
=============================================================

CRITICAL CONTEXT
----------------
- Book's case study: 6,402 TA rules tested on S&P 500 (Nov 1980 - Jul 2005)
- Result: ZERO rules statistically significant after data-mining bias correction
  - Best rule (E-12-28-10-30): 10.25% annualized return, p-value = 0.8164 (FAR above 0.05)
  - Needed p < 0.05 to be significant; needed >15% return for significance vs >17% for high significance
  - Using naive single-rule significance test: 320 of 6,402 looked significant → pure chance
- ALL tested rules were on S&P 500 index, NOT FX
- Methods: White's Reality Check (WRC) + Monte Carlo Permutation (MCP), both correcting for data-mining bias
- Statistical significance threshold used: α = 0.05

STRATEGIES / INDICATORS PROVEN TO WORK
=============================================================

STRATEGY 1: Trend-Following Channel Breakout in Commodity Futures (INCLUDES FX FUTURES)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Indicator: N-Period Channel Breakout (Donchian-style)
Rule (complete):
  - Buy (go long): price closes ABOVE the highest close of past N days
  - Sell (go short): price closes BELOW the lowest close of past N days
  - N tested: 3, 5, 8, 12, 18, 27, 41, 61, 91, 137, 205 days
  - Channel boundaries use PAST N periods NOT including current day

Statistical Evidence:
  - Kestner study (1990-2001): Sharpe ratio for trend-following in FUTURES = 0.604
  - Kestner study: Sharpe ratio for trend-following in STOCKS = 0.046
  - Ratio: Futures Sharpe 13× higher than stocks
  - Markets tested: 29 commodities in 8 sectors INCLUDING FX (Australian, British, Canadian,
    German, Swiss, Japanese currencies)
  - 5 trend-following systems tested: channel breakout, dual moving-average crossover,
    two momentum versions, MACD vs signal line
  - MLM Index (12-month MA crossover on 25 commodities): Sharpe ratio comparable or
    better than equity indices since the 1960s
  - Donchian 4-week rule: best of several systems tested on 16 years × 7 commodities (1970 Dunn & Hargitt)
  - Channel breakout had HIGHEST risk-adjusted performance vs 4 other methods on Eurodollars 1985-1994

Works on FX? YES - explicitly tested on FX futures (GBP, AUD, CAD, etc.)
Recommended Timeframe: Multi-day to multi-month (original studies daily bars; MLM uses monthly)
  For H1 EURUSD: adapt to 20-80 bar lookback (equivalent to 1-4 trading days)
  Warn: Most evidence is on daily/weekly timeframes

Pitfalls / Failure Conditions:
  - Loses 50-65% of trades (only works when trends occur; markets trend minority of time)
  - Max drawdowns up to 30% in ranging markets
  - Requires adequate capitalization to survive drawdowns
  - Trend systems in stocks have Sharpe ~0.05 (essentially worthless in equities)
  - Data-mining bias: MANY lookback parameters tested → must use walk-forward or WRC correction
  - As markets get more efficient, returns decay (Kidd & Borsen 2004)

FTMO Applicability: YES - but H1 timeframe may generate too many false signals;
  Daily channel breakout preferred; 5% max DD constraint requires tight position sizing

STRATEGY 2: Momentum (6-12 Month Price Momentum - Cross-Sectional Stocks)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Indicator: 6-12 month rate of return momentum (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993)
Rule (partial):
  - Rank assets by prior 6-12 month return
  - Long top decile, short bottom decile
  - Hold 6 months, rebalance monthly

Statistical Evidence:
  - Jegadeesh & Titman (1993): annualized return spread = 10% (long top – short bottom decile)
  - Effect confirmed even by EMH advocate Fama: "past returns predict future returns"
  - NOT tested on FX in Aronson directly; applies to cross-sectional stock selection

Works on FX? PARTIAL - theory applies; Aronson cites it for stocks, not directly tested on FX pairs
  (cross-sectional momentum across currency pairs is a known FX anomaly in other literature)
Recommended Timeframe: 6-12 month lookback; monthly rebalance
Pitfalls: Reverses at 3-5 year horizon (De Bondt & Thaler: -8%/yr if held too long);
  Short-term 1-month reversal exists; does not transfer to single pair prediction

=============================================================

STRATEGY 3: Short-Term Mean Reversion / Liquidity Premium (Stocks - NOT directly FX)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Indicator: 2-week negative price + declining volume (Cooper 1999)
Rule:
  - Stock declined sharply over prior 2 weeks
  - Decline accompanied by declining (not increasing) volume
  - Signal: price likely to rise over following week
  - Declining volume = distressed sellers; insufficient buyers = temporary underpricing

Statistical Evidence:
  - Cooper walk-forward study (1978-1993): annualized return = 44.95% vs benchmark 17.91%
  - Qualified by walk-forward OOS simulation - NOT data mined
  - Long/short portfolio of 300 large-cap stocks

Works on FX? NOT directly tested; applies to stocks only per Aronson
  Concept (liquidity premium) may apply to FX during illiquid sessions
Recommended Timeframe: 2-week lookback, 1-week holding period (daily bars)
Pitfalls: FX has continuous 24hr liquidity unlike stocks; volume data less reliable on FX;
  Not tested on currency pairs

=============================================================

STRATEGY 4: MLM 12-Month Moving Average Crossover (Commodity/FX Futures Benchmark)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Indicator: 12-month simple moving average
Rule (complete):
  - At month-end: if price of nearby futures > 12-month SMA → LONG next month
  - If price < 12-month SMA → SHORT next month
  - Applied to 25 commodity markets including FX futures
  - One-time monthly decision; hold entire month

Statistical Evidence:
  - Mt. Lucas Management (MLM) Index: Sharpe ratio comparable to or better than equity indices
  - Historical record back to 1960s
  - This IS the benchmark; represents returns achievable with NO special skill
  - Jensen, Johnson & Mercer (2002): documented risk-premium explanation

Works on FX? YES - FX futures (AUD, GBP, CAD, CHF, JPY, EUR) included in MLM Index
Recommended Timeframe: Monthly bars only (as tested); daily equivalent = ~250-bar SMA crossover
  H1 equivalent: ~6000 bars (not practical)

Pitfalls: Monthly timeframe only; not applicable to H1 directly;
  Represents minimum-skill benchmark, not optimized strategy;
  Returns may decay as futures markets become more efficient

=============================================================

STRATEGY 5: 52-Week High Momentum (Stocks Only - Anchoring Effect)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Indicator: Proximity to 52-week high
Rule:
  - Rank stocks by proximity to 52-week high
  - Long stocks near/at 52-week high (anchoring prevents full repricing)
  - Does NOT reverse like 6-12 month momentum reversal

Statistical Evidence:
  - George & Hwang (2004): superior momentum profits, no reversal at 3-5 year horizon
  - Profits greater than standard 6-12 month momentum strategy
  - Explanation: investor anchoring to visible price level delays price discovery

Works on FX? NOT directly tested; 52-week high breakout concept applicable to FX pairs
Recommended Timeframe: Weekly/Monthly rank; daily execution
Pitfalls: Designed for cross-sectional stock selection; single-pair application untested;
  Marshall & Hodges: strategy "not profitable outside US" in some non-US markets

WHAT DOES NOT WORK (PROVEN FAILURES)
=============================================================

1. HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS PATTERN
   - Chang & Osler: objectively defined H&S, tested on stocks and 6 currencies
   - Result: "worthless on stocks, only modestly profitable on currencies"
   - Profitable in only 2 of 6 currencies tested
   - The simple zigzag filter OUTPERFORMED H&S in every test
   - H&S adds ZERO incremental value when combined with zigzag signals
   - Verdict for FX traders: DO NOT USE H&S as signal; use zigzag breakout if anything

2. ELLIOTT WAVE PRINCIPLE
   - Aronson explicit: "not a legitimate theory but a story"
   - Rules too loosely defined to be falsifiable → untestable → scientifically invalid
   - Analogy to pre-Copernican epicycles: fits past perfectly but no predictive power
   - Verdict: REJECT entirely; no statistical evidence supports it

3. TRADITIONAL SUBJECTIVE CHART PATTERNS (general)
   - Untestable by definition (no objective entry/exit rules)
   - Expert forecasters perform at correlation 0.33 vs statistical models at 0.64
   - Statistical models 3.76× more informative than human experts (Camerer meta-analysis)
   - No evidence subjective pattern identification generates edge

4. ALL 6,402 SIMPLE INDIVIDUAL RULES ON S&P 500 (comprehensive)
   - Themes tested: trends, extremes/transitions, divergences
   - Operators: channel breakout, moving average, channel normalization (stochastics)
   - Additional series: market breadth, on-balance volume, bond yields, interest rate spreads
   - NONE achieved statistical significance (p < 0.05) after data-mining bias correction
   - Best rule had p-value = 0.8164 (random noise)
   - Note: This was on S&P 500 specifically; another study (Hsu & Kuan 2005) found
     statistically significant rules for NASDAQ and Russell 2000 but NOT S&P 500 or Dow

5. TREND-FOLLOWING IN STOCKS
   - Kestner: Sharpe ratio 0.046 across 5 trend systems on stocks (essentially zero)
   - vs Sharpe 0.604 for same systems on futures
   - Verdict: Trend following applied to stock indices does not work reliably

6. SMALL-CAP EFFECT (for reference)
   - Was 9%/year premium pre-1980s
   - "Predictive power has disappeared since the mid to late 1980s" (Aronson p.355)
   - Example of anomalies being arbitraged away over time

STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE THRESHOLDS (Aronson's framework)
=============================================================

- Standard α = 0.05 (5%) for single rule test (null: rule has no edge)
- After data-mining bias correction with WRC/MCP:
  - For best of 6,402 rules: need >15% annualized return to reach p < 0.05
  - Need >17% annualized return for p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- When testing multiple rules: use White's Reality Check or Monte Carlo Permutation
- Naive single-rule t-test on best of N rules grossly overstates significance
  (best rule had p=0.0005 naive vs p=0.8164 corrected → 1,600× distortion)
- For your H1 backtests: With ~5 years H1 data ≈ 26,000 bars; adequate sample size
- Key principle: if you tested 100 parameter combinations, your "best" result needs
  WRC-corrected p-value; naive p-value is meaningless

ACTIONABLE CONCLUSIONS FOR FTMO EURUSD/GBPUSD H1
=============================================================

1. USE: N-period channel breakout (trend-following) - has strongest theoretical
   AND empirical backing for FX futures specifically. Test lookbacks 20-80 bars on H1.
   Expect 35-50% win rate; need large wins vs small losses (R:R > 2:1).

2. USE (carefully): Momentum / trend following with MA crossover. MLM uses 12-month
   MA (daily). H1 equivalent unclear; stick to 200-bar+ on H1 or use daily bars.

3. AVOID: Head-and-shoulders pattern (statistically invalid on FX)
4. AVOID: Elliott Wave (unfalsifiable, no evidence)
5. AVOID: Stochastics/oscillators alone (tested extensively in 6,402 rules → failed)
6. AVOID: Divergence rules alone (failed in case study)

CRITICAL METHODOLOGY WARNINGS:
- ANY strategy found by scanning parameters MUST be walk-forward tested OOS
- With 5 years H1 EURUSD data, test period → 3 years train / 2 years OOS minimum
- If you test >20 parameter combinations, apply data-mining bias correction
- Target: >15% annualized return on detrended data for statistical confidence
- With 5% max DD constraint: position size must be tiny; channel breakout with 30% DD
  potential is incompatible unless position size is ≤5% of account risk per trade

TIMEFRAME REALITY CHECK:
- All empirical evidence in Aronson is DAILY bars (stocks, futures)
- H1 bars = 6× noisier than daily
- Channel breakout evidence is strongest on daily+ timeframes
- For H1: need proportionally longer lookbacks OR accept lower statistical confidence
- Recommend: use daily bars for signal generation even with H1 execution capability
